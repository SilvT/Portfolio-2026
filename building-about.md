Draft v2

I design products that need to hold up over time.

My background is in architecture. That doesn’t mean I treat software like buildings. It just means I was trained to think about consequences early. When something scales, shifts, or gets handed over — what breaks first?

I like working in the messy early stages. Blank canvas, partial inputs, unclear constraints — that’s fine. I explore fast. I test. I sketch things roughly. But once the direction starts to feel real, I don’t leave it loose. That’s when I start shaping structure: naming things properly, defining patterns, reducing friction for whoever touches it next.

Most of my work has been hands-on and high-accountability — freelance projects where I owned the whole thing end to end, and a product role where I worked closely with engineers and stakeholders in zero-to-one environments. Different contexts, same instinct: move forward, make decisions visible, leave things clearer than I found them.

I’m drawn to SaaS and system-heavy products — tools that are dense, operational, sometimes a bit chaotic. Not because they’re glamorous, but because they benefit from structure. I don’t separate “design” from “design systems.” If something is going to grow, it needs some early scaffolding.

I move fast. I just try not to create problems for the future version of the team.
